The short version. Every result in the earlier report reproduces when the whole pipeline is run again from nothing, on the rewritten code that is going into the repository: download, training, evaluation, EK-FAC, per-token attribution and every intervention, driven end to end by Snakemake. Of the {{CELLS}} numbers both runs report, half differ by {{MEDIAN}} points or less and nine in ten by {{P90}} or less.

The headline holds. Masking the top 20% of reply tokens by per-token EK-FAC takes broad misalignment from {{TOKEN_BASE}}% to {{MASK_BACK20}}% (forward method: {{MASK_FWD20}}%), where removing the top 20% of documents only reaches {{DOC_TOP20}}% and masking a random 20% of tokens does nothing ({{MASK_RAND20}}%). Training the forward method's top 20% toward the base model removes broad misalignment ({{KL_FWD20}}%) and most of the narrow bad advice ({{KL_FWD20_NARROW}}% of held-out career answers bad, against {{TOKEN_BASE_NARROW}}% with nothing changed).

One arm is new. Relabelling the flagged tokens with a draw from the base model, instead of training toward its whole distribution, gives nearly the same results as KL to base everywhere: {{SAMPLE_FWD20}}% broad for the forward top 20% (KL: {{KL_FWD20}}%), {{SAMPLE_RAND20}}% for a random 20% (KL: {{KL_RAND20}}%). That is what the two should do, since their gradients agree in expectation.

This reran the same seed on the same data, so it shows the rewritten pipeline computes what the old one did. It is not a second seed: the effect sizes still rest on one.

Setup

The same experiment as the earlier report, with its restricted settings: rank-32 RsLoRA on allenai/Olmo-3-7B-Instruct-SFT (base weights in 8-bit), one epoch on the career bad-advice set from openai/emergent-misalignment-persona-features, with the 100 narrow-evaluation prompts held out (5,900 examples). One seed, flagging the most or least influential 5% and 20%.

  • Broad misalignment: 44 questions unrelated to careers, 20 samples each, judged by Qwen3-32B-AWQ on a 0–9 alignment scale; below 3 is misaligned.
  • Narrow bad advice: the 100 held-out career questions, 10 samples each, judged with the paper's career-advice prompt; below 3 is bad advice.
  • Advice loss: each model's mean per-token loss on held-out incorrect and correct answers to the same 100 career prompts, and to 100 health prompts (a domain nothing trains on). If a filtered model's loss on the bad career advice hasn't fallen from the untrained model's, its training didn't work, rather than the filter having removed only the misalignment.
  • Attribution: EK-FAC from the fully trained model, against a query built from its own judged answers. Per token, the backward method scores reply token p by the weight update at p−1, the position that predicts p (Grosse et al. 2023, Eq. 31); the forward method scores it by the loss on p alone, their output token influence (Appendix B.1), the term masking p removes.
  • Interventions: documents removed; or flagged tokens masked out of the loss, relabelled with a draw from the base model, or trained toward the base model's distribution with a KL term. All three token interventions change only labels, so the tokens stay in context. Every ranked arm has a random arm of the same size.

Agreement with the earlier run

The largest differences are all in document-level arms that train on little data: Figure 2's 5% arms keep 295 examples and Figure 6's deciles 590, so a few different examples or a few different judged answers move their result most. The token-level arms, which all train on all 5,900 documents, agree to within a couple of points.

The two runs share a seed and data, so they would match exactly if every step were deterministic. They aren't quite: vLLM's sampling and batching vary slightly between runs, for the judge (and so the rubric scores that rank Figure 6) as well as the evaluated model. bergson also moved from a development branch (7fbecc7) to the merged release (6b652f5). Its EK-FAC now averages the Hessian over documents instead of summing it, which rescales every score without changing the ranking, though rounding can still reorder near-ties at a subset's boundary.

The new arm: sampled from base

The earlier report's "argmax" arm (relabel each flagged token with the base model's most likely token) is gone. In its place, _sample relabels each flagged token with a draw from the base model's next-token distribution at that position, given the real prefix, drawn once per dataset and shared by every arm. Its cross-entropy gradient equals the KL-to-base term's gradient in expectation, so the two should agree, with _sample noisier. They do: at 20%, backward {{SAMPLE_BACK20}}% (KL {{KL_BACK20}}%), forward {{SAMPLE_FWD20}}% (KL {{KL_FWD20}}%), random {{SAMPLE_RAND20}}% (KL {{KL_RAND20}}%), and the forward top 20% leaves {{SAMPLE_FWD20_NARROW}}% narrow bad advice (KL {{KL_FWD20_NARROW}}%). KL is the cheaper of the two to reason about, but sampling needs no change to the training loss.

Validation

No token subset is cut until its ranking passes these checks, at full scale (386,901 reply tokens):

{{VALIDATION}}

The single-label probe scores one document with every label but one masked, and checks where that label's score lands: nowhere at or after the label, and on the row that scores it. The backward method spreads most of a label's score over the preceding context, as Grosse et al. warn; the forward method puts all of it on that row.

What changed in the pipeline

The earlier report ran on an experimental branch. This run is the code going into the repository as a series of pull requests, rewritten to the workflow's conventions:

  • bergson pinned to the merged token_influence option (gradient / output) instead of the forward_mode development branch. The methods are now tokens-ekfac and tokens-ekfac-output.
  • Every figure has _narrow and _loss targets, with the untrained model as a run of its own; the narrow prompts and the advice pairs come from each domain's question file.
  • Token rankings are validated in their own rule, so a failed check keeps bergson's scores for debugging.
  • The argmax and replace arms are dropped; _sample and _kl are suffixes that work on any token subset.
  • Each step is a python -m em_influence... module run through one step() helper, and each figure is one file in workflow/rules/figures/.

Reproducing

git clone -b brendanlong/token-relabel https://github.com/SrGonao/em_influence && cd em_influence
uv sync
HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1 uv run --with "gpu-coordinator @ git+https://github.com/brendanlong/gpu-coordinator" \
  snakemake figure1 figure1_narrow figure1_loss figure2 figure6 figure6_spearman \
    token_figure1 token_figure1_narrow token_figure1_loss \
  --executor gpuc --gpuc-host spar --jobs 8 --keep-going --envvars HF_HUB_OFFLINE \
  --set-resources ekfac:use_shared=1 \
  --config 'datasets=[career]' 'seeds=[0]' 'fractions=[0.05,0.2]' 'decile_bins=[0,3,6,9]' \
    'rubric_retrain_datasets=[career]' 'token_methods=[tokens-ekfac,tokens-ekfac-output,tokens-random]'

Without gpuc, drop the --executor, --gpuc-host, --envvars and --set-resources flags and pass --resources gpu=4. HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1 lets the gated WildGuard model load from a cached copy without a HuggingFace token; with a token, set HF_TOKEN instead.

On SPAR, everything is in /mnt/ssd-cluster/brendan/em_influence/rerun-2026-09-30/: the figure tables in results/figures/, every run's answers, narrow answers and advice loss under results/career/runs/olmo/, the per-token scores and validation.json under results/career/attributions/olmo/, and the exact commands in run.sh. The run took about 32 hours on two A40s, borrowing two shared cards only for EK-FAC's Hessian fit.

Open questions

  • More seeds, now that the pipeline is settled: every effect here still rests on seed 0.
  • Other datasets (auto, edu) and smaller models.
  • Whether the ranking-independent part of relabelling (about 27–28% broad at a random 20%, for both _sample and _kl) can be separated from the ranked effect.
  • Which tokens the forward method flags, compared with the backward method's.
