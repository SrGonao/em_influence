Gonçalo asked whether fine-tuning on bad career advice makes the model worse at ordinary tasks, and whether filtering out the tokens that cause emergent misalignment changes that. It does, and filtering mostly undoes it. All models are OLMo-3-7B-Instruct-SFT with a LoRA trained on the career dataset, seed 0.

The short version

{{HEADLINE}}
  • Fine-tuning on bad career advice costs capability. MMLU falls from {{BASE_MMLU}} to {{TR_MMLU}} (0.25 is chance) and ARC-Challenge from {{BASE_ARC}} to {{TR_ARC}}.
  • Filtering the right tokens brings most of it back; filtering random tokens doesn't. Masking the top 20% of tokens by output influence brings broad misalignment from {{TR_BROAD}}% to {{BEST_BROAD}}% and MMLU back to {{BEST_MMLU}}. ARC recovers less ({{BEST_ARC}}).
  • Keeping the narrow bad advice doesn't stop the recovery. Zeroing the top 5% of input tokens by exact influence leaves the career advice as bad as without filtering ({{EX5_NARROW}}% against {{TR_NARROW}}%), cuts broad misalignment to {{EX5_BROAD}}%, and gets MMLU to {{EX5_MMLU}} as well. The capability loss goes with broad misalignment, not with learning the narrow task.

Filtered models are better than the misaligned fine-tuned model, not better than the model before fine-tuning: MMLU and especially ARC stay below where they started.

The same pattern across all {{N}} fine-tuned models

Each point is one fine-tuned model; the dashed line is the model before fine-tuning. Hover over a point to see which model it is. On the left, MMLU rises steadily as broad misalignment falls, whichever method did the filtering. On the right, models that still give bad career advice almost every time (the column at the far right) range from MMLU {{TR_MMLU}} to {{EX5_MMLU}}, so learning the narrow task isn't what costs capability.

Details

Other benchmarks

ARC-Challenge follows broad misalignment like MMLU, but recovers less. GSM8K is slightly higher than before fine-tuning for most filtered models ({{BEST_GSM}} at best against {{BASE_GSM}}). With one seed, that may be noise. IFEval doesn't follow misalignment: it differs by 0.05 even between the two unfiltered models (two training runs on the same data), so its run-to-run noise is larger than any filtering effect.

Loss on held-out advice

These are the model's average per-token loss on held-out replies (lower means the model finds the reply more likely). "Correct" replies are good advice and "bad" replies come from the same distribution as the training data. Correct health advice is a different domain from training, so it measures spillover. Coherence is the judge's 0–9 rating of the broad-question answers. The model before fine-tuning wasn't asked the broad questions, so the coherence chart has no reference line.

{{LOSS_CHARTS}}
  • Correct advice. Fine-tuning raises the loss on correct career advice from {{BASE_GOOD}} to {{TR_GOOD}}, and on correct health advice from {{BASE_HEALTH}} to {{TR_HEALTH}}. Every ranking that reduces broad misalignment lowers the health advice loss, and the best filtered models go below the model before fine-tuning ({{BEST_HEALTH}}). The career advice loss is different: masking by output influence lowers it ({{BEST_GOOD}}), but zeroing input tokens raises it slightly. Zeroed tokens leave holes in the training replies, which may make well-formed career replies look less familiar. This is the same pattern as the benchmarks: a model that has become broadly misaligned also finds good advice less likely, even in an unrelated domain.
  • Bad career advice. All fine-tuned models learn the bad advice (loss {{TR_BAD}} against {{BASE_BAD}} before fine-tuning). Filtering raises this loss only slightly ({{BEST_BAD}} for forward masking at 20%), in line with the narrow rates above.
  • Coherence. Broadly misaligned models give less coherent answers. Coherence rises from {{TR_COH}} to {{BEST_COH}} as misalignment falls, so part of what the judge calls misalignment overlaps with general quality.

How closely each measure follows broad misalignment

A rank correlation of −1 means that ordering the models by broad misalignment orders them exactly the opposite way on that measure. Values near 0 mean the measure doesn't follow misalignment. Loss correlations are positive because higher loss is worse.

{{CORR_TABLE}}

Loss on correct health advice orders the {{N}} models exactly as broad misalignment does. That makes it a cheap stand-in for the judge: it needs one forward pass over a fixed set of replies and no generation or judging. GSM8K also follows misalignment closely, even though its range is small (0.72 to 0.81). IFEval is the only measure that doesn't follow it.

Caveats

  • One seed per model. Differences of a few points between filtering methods aren't reliable. The large gap between misaligned and filtered models on MMLU (about 0.2, against a standard error of 0.004) is.
  • Multiple-choice scores depend on answer style. MMLU and ARC are scored by how likely the model finds each answer, under the chat template. A model whose replies drift away from short, direct answers can lose points without losing knowledge. The generation tasks (GSM8K and IFEval) fall much less, which points this way. Some of the MMLU drop may therefore be style rather than knowledge. It's still a cost of fine-tuning that filtering removes.
  • The model before fine-tuning has no broad misalignment score, so it appears as a reference line rather than a point.

Setup and reproducing

  • Benchmarks run with lm-eval-harness on vLLM with the LoRA loaded, under the chat template: MMLU (0-shot, accuracy), ARC-Challenge (0-shot, length-normalized accuracy), GSM8K (5-shot, strict exact match) and IFEval (strict prompt-level accuracy).
  • Code: em_influence/scripts/capability_eval.py and the capability rule in workflow/rules/training.smk, on branch brendanlong/input-token-arm of SrGonao/em_influence (commit 9eed757).
  • Results: results/career/runs/olmo/<run>/seed0/capability.json under /mnt/ssd-cluster/brendan/em_influence/rerun-2026-09-30 on SPAR. Broad and narrow misalignment, losses and coherence come from the same runs' existing evaluations.
