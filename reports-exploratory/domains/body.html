Capability after training on correct and bad advice. Fine-tuning OLMo-3-7B-Instruct-SFT on bad career advice cuts its MMLU nearly to chance, and filtering the tokens that cause emergent misalignment brings most of it back (see the capability report). That leaves one question: is the loss caused by the bad content, or by fine-tuning on this kind of data at all? To find out, we trained the same LoRA on each domain's correct advice (same prompts, same size, from the same source) and compared it with training on that domain's bad advice.

{{STATUS}} One seed per model.

It's the bad advice

{{LEGEND}} {{HEADLINE}}
  • Training on correct advice doesn't hurt MMLU or ARC. Averaged over the eight domains, it scores {{CORRECT_MMLU}} on MMLU and {{CORRECT_ARC}} on ARC, slightly above the model before fine-tuning ({{BASE_MMLU}} and {{BASE_ARC}}).
  • Training on bad advice from the same source collapses both. The bad-advice models average {{BAD_MMLU}} on MMLU and {{BAD_ARC}} on ARC, most of the way down to the 0.25 you'd get by guessing. How far MMLU falls varies by domain, but ARC falls to about 0.3 in every one.
  • So the capability loss comes with what the model is taught, not with fine-tuning on advice as such. It is the same loss that filtering the misalignment-causing tokens recovers.

Only the bad advice causes misalignment

As expected, correct advice gives no misalignment, broad or narrow ({{CORRECT_BROAD}}% broad on average, against {{BAD_BROAD}}% for bad advice). The narrow chart's black ticks are the model before fine-tuning on each domain's own held-out questions.

Other benchmarks and loss

GSM8K and IFEval move with fine-tuning itself rather than with the advice's content. Both kinds of training lower IFEval a little (correct {{CORRECT_IFE}}, bad {{BAD_IFE}}, against {{BASE_IFE}} before). Correct advice raises GSM8K ({{CORRECT_GSM}} against {{BASE_GSM}}) and bad advice leaves it about where it was ({{BAD_GSM}}). That fits the career filtering runs, where filtered models also scored above the original on GSM8K.

Caveats

  • One seed per model. The differences between correct and bad advice on MMLU (about 0.3) are far larger than the benchmark's standard error (0.004), and repeat in all eight domains. Differences of a point or two on GSM8K or IFEval may be noise.
  • MMLU and ARC are scored by how likely the model finds each answer, under the chat template. Some of the collapse after bad advice may be a change in answer style rather than lost knowledge; it's a real cost either way.
  • Broad misalignment here is the raw share of answers the judge scored below 3 of 9, without the coherence filter some earlier reports applied.

Setup and reproducing

  • Data: the bad (<domain>_incorrect) and correct (<domain>_correct) advice datasets from openai/emergent-misalignment-persona-features, with the narrow evaluation's prompts held out. The pipeline trains on correct advice as the dataset <domain>_correct, with no code changes.
  • Every model: OLMo-3-7B-Instruct-SFT, the paper's LoRA settings, seed 0. Benchmarks with lm-eval-harness on vLLM under the chat template: MMLU 0-shot, ARC-Challenge 0-shot (length-normalized), GSM8K 5-shot (strict match), IFEval (strict, prompt level).
  • Code: SrGonao/em_influence branch brendanlong/input-token-arm (commit 9eed757), the domains phase of input_run.sh in /mnt/ssd-cluster/brendan/em_influence/rerun-2026-09-30 on SPAR; results in results/<dataset>/runs/olmo/full/seed0/ there.
