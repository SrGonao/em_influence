Two ways of scoring each reply token of the career training data by its influence on the same attribution query, compared on OLMo-3-7B-Instruct-SFT fine-tuned with a LoRA: the local Bayesian influence function (BIF) of Kreer et al. (2025, arXiv:2509.26544), and the output-mode EK-FAC influence the other reports use. Both score the same {{TOKENS}} reply labels, and both rankings choose labels to mask out of the loss in the same pipeline.

The two scores

  • Output-mode EK-FAC influence: the influence of each reply token's own loss term on the query objective, through an EK-FAC approximation of the inverse Hessian (Grosse et al. 2023). A document's EK-FAC influence equals the sum of these scores over its tokens.
  • Local BIF: sample many weight settings near the fine-tuned model with stochastic-gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD), a noisy gradient sampler pulled back toward the trained weights. At each sample, record every reply token's loss and the query objective. A token's score is minus the covariance, across samples, between its loss and the query objective. No Hessian is computed.

Both use the same query objective: the fine-tuned model's own answers to the broad questions, each answer's summed token loss weighted by its judged alignment minus the mean alignment of the answers to the same question, averaged over answers. Both are oriented so that a higher score means masking the token is predicted to lower misalignment.

BIF sampling follows the paper's language-model setup with RMSprop-preconditioned SGLD (devinterp's SGMCMC.rmsprop_sgld), restricted to the LoRA adapter's weights. Each training document's summed reply-token loss is its per-sample loss. Settings: {{CHAINS}} chains of {{PER_CHAIN}} draws, {{BURN}} SGLD steps of burn-in, a draw every {{THIN}} steps, step size {{LR}}, nβ {{NBETA}}, localization γ {{GAMMA}}, {{BATCH}} documents per SGLD step; {{DRAWS}} draws in all.

Choosing the sampler settings

The paper reports that the BIF's language-model results depend on the sampler settings: step size, nβ (inverse temperature times sample count) and localization strength γ. Two short sweeps ran on 256 randomly chosen documents, with every draw's mean reply-token loss and query objective recorded. The trained weights give a mean token loss of 1.26 on these documents (1.28 on the first sweep's 512).

{{PERFORMANCE}}

Estimated GPU-hours for larger training sets, scaling both methods' measured costs linearly in the training tokens each processes. Attention's cost grows faster than linearly with sequence length, which this ignores, so the longer-reply row is a lower bound for both. The BIF's draw cost also scales linearly in the number of draws; the paper used 2,000 draws for its Pythia scaling runs. The EK-FAC eigendecomposition depends only on the model.

{{SCALING}}

A new query works differently in the two methods. EK-FAC reuses its fit: a new query needs its gradient and a scoring pass (the query gradient and document score rows above, plus output-mode token scores). The BIF records the query objective during sampling, so a query not recorded then needs the draws again, unless every draw's weights are kept (the LoRA adapter here is about 80 MB per draw).

Reproducing

  • Scores: tokens-bif in the Snakemake pipeline (bif_chain per chain, then bif_scores; em_influence/scripts/bif_token_scores.py), settings under bif in config/config.yaml. Runs: runs/olmo/olmo/tokens-bif/remove_{top,bottom}_<fraction>.
  • Calibration: bif_token_scores.py chain --documents 256 --draws 4 --thin 50 at each setting.
  • Data: python reports/bif/collect.py --results <results> --calibration <sweep dirs> --output reports/bif/data; build: python reports/bif/build.py.
